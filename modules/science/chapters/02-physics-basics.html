Physics — Forces, Energy & Motion

Chapter 2 · 10 min read · Science Module

Physics is the science of how the universe works at the most fundamental level. Everything that moves, every source of heat and light, every structure that stands — all of it operates by physical rules. Once you understand the basics, you start seeing physics everywhere.

What Is a Force?

A force is a push or a pull. Forces change the motion of objects. When you push a book across a table, you apply a force. When a ball falls, gravity applies a force. When two magnets repel each other, that is also a force.

Forces are measured in Newtons (N). One Newton is roughly the force of gravity on a 100-gram apple (about the weight of a small apple falling into your hand).

Newton's Three Laws of Motion

Isaac Newton published these in 1687. They still describe almost everything in everyday physics.

Law 1 — Inertia: Objects Resist Change

"An object at rest stays at rest, and an object in motion stays in motion, unless acted upon by an external force."

What this means: Objects do not change what they are doing unless something forces them to. A book sitting on a table stays there. A ball rolling on a frictionless surface keeps rolling forever. In real life, friction is usually the force that stops moving objects.

Why it matters: This is why you feel pushed back into your seat when a car accelerates. Your body wants to stay still; the seat forces it forward. It is why wearing a seatbelt matters — your body has inertia and will keep moving forward in a crash even after the car stops.

Law 2 — Force = Mass × Acceleration

F = ma

A larger force accelerates an object more. A heavier object requires more force to accelerate at the same rate. To push a shopping cart twice as fast, you need to apply twice the force. A loaded shopping cart needs more force than an empty one to achieve the same acceleration.

Practical note: Mass is not the same as weight. Mass is how much matter something contains (measured in kilograms). Weight is the gravitational force on that mass. On the moon, your mass is the same as on Earth, but your weight is one-sixth because gravity is weaker there.

Law 3 — Every Action Has an Equal and Opposite Reaction

"For every action, there is an equal and opposite reaction."

When you push against a wall, the wall pushes back with equal force. When a rocket expels gas downward, the gas pushes the rocket upward. When you jump, you push down on the ground and the ground pushes you up. Forces always come in pairs.

Energy

Energy is the ability to do work or cause change. The most important rule about energy is that it cannot be created or destroyed — only converted from one form to another. This is the Law of Conservation of Energy.

Kinetic Energy — Energy of Motion

Kinetic energy is the energy an object has because it is moving. A baseball flying through the air has kinetic energy. A moving car has kinetic energy. When the car brakes, that kinetic energy is converted to heat in the brake pads — it is not destroyed.

Potential Energy — Stored Energy

Potential energy is stored energy waiting to be released. A stretched rubber band has elastic potential energy. A boulder at the top of a hill has gravitational potential energy. When it rolls down, that potential energy converts to kinetic energy.

Energy Conversion in Real Life

  • A power plant burns fuel (chemical energy) → heat energy → steam → mechanical energy → electrical energy
  • Your body converts food (chemical energy) → movement (kinetic energy) + heat
  • Solar panels convert light energy → electrical energy
  • In every conversion, some energy is lost as heat — no conversion is 100% efficient

Gravity

Gravity is a force of attraction between objects with mass. Every object with mass pulls on every other object with mass. The more mass, the stronger the pull. The farther apart, the weaker the pull.

On Earth's surface, gravity gives all objects the same acceleration downward: 9.8 meters per second per second (written as 9.8 m/s²). A feather and a hammer dropped in a vacuum fall at exactly the same rate — as astronaut David Scott famously demonstrated on the moon in 1971.

Key Takeaway

Physics is the most fundamental science — chemistry, biology, and engineering all rest on it. Newton's three laws and the concept of energy explain almost everything you experience daily: why cars brake, why rockets fly, why your muscles fatigue. Learn these foundations well and every other science becomes more intuitive.
